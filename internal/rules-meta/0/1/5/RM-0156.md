The audit's invariant 5 normalizes a rule bullet by stripping
backticks and bold but KEEPS parentheses, and the search needle is
the quote's first five words — so a parenthetical or backtick phrase
sitting between a bullet's opening words makes the meta's quote
prefix unfindable even though all the words are present. The failure
is invisible at draft time — the author sees every word present — and
surfaces only when the audit joins the bullet's literal bytes. First
observed 2026-11-15 in the founding rules-meta wave: a new bullet
read "… is English (the AGENTS.md language rule) and …", its meta
quote spanned the parenthetical, and the first audit run failed
invariant 5 (record: issue #540 §2).

Check: sample metas across the tree and recompute each quote from
its rule bullet's joined first five words — parentheticals and
punctuation included, backticks and bold stripped (the
character-normalized form the audit matches).

The character-level half stayed implicit in the RULE TEXT until
pr-00589 (2026-11-17) — this meta's first paragraph stated the
mechanics since creation, but the rule bullet a wave author reads did
not: the audit's needle extraction keeps the quote's literal bytes while
the target-side normalization strips backticks and bold, so a quote
written with a backtick inside its first five words can never match —
the release-PR-track meta's draft red inv5 exactly this way, the
first quote ever to carry a backtick. The rule text now states the
needle is character-normalized (drop the characters, keep the words),
matching what this meta's own needle already practiced.

Source: issue #540 §2 (end-of-session reflection over PR #539);
needle normalization per issue #591 (pr-00589 wave).
