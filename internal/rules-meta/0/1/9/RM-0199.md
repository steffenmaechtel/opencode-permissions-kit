The probe rule named the WHAT and WHEN (probe before the verdict
posts) but not the WHO — and reviewer checkpoints can carry
"empirically probed on this host" assertions that are wrong. PR #729
round a (2026-11-21): two of three reviewer axes asserted the same
bracket-expression bypass mechanism as MED, one checkpoint claiming an
empirical case-probe match under dash AND bash; the main session's own
scratch probes showed no match (backslash escapes inside bracket
expressions), the end-to-end run of the shipped function printed the
loud refusal, and the wrong MED was retracted out of the findings
table — it stayed visible in the round comment's retracted section,
as the retraction rule's shape demands. Had verification treated
the reviewer's probe claim as discharging the duty, a fix wave
would have reshaped a correct pattern.

Check: a round comment confirming or retracting a shell-semantics
finding names the probe artifact in the session's own scratch — a
reviewer's reported probe appears only as a lead-in, never as the
evidence.

Source: issue #737 (PR #729 round a).
